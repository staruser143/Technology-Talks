**Scenario**

A mental-health support nonprofit runs a Claude-powered intake assistant that has an extended conversation with a person seeking to connect with counseling resources — conversations can run 30+ turns as the assistant gathers context, discusses options, and helps the person think through what kind of support fits their situation. Early in the conversation (turn 3), the person mentions they're currently taking a specific medication that interacts dangerously with certain therapeutic approaches, and the assistant correctly notes this and adjusts its recommendations accordingly for the next several turns.

The engineering team's current design relies entirely on the conversation's natural history plus periodic auto-compaction to manage the growing context as the conversation extends past 30 turns — there is no separate mechanism that extracts and protects specific critical facts outside the flowing conversation. By turn 34, after two compaction cycles have already occurred, the person asks a follow-up question that would normally trigger the assistant to suggest a specific therapeutic approach — but the medication interaction detail from turn 3, having gone through two rounds of automatic summarization, wasn't preserved in either summary (each compaction pass judged other, more recent content as more salient), and the assistant makes a recommendation without accounting for it.

**Question**: What's the core design flaw here, and what should the team do differently?

A) The core flaw is that auto-compaction is unreliable software and should be disabled entirely, with the team reverting to sending the full, ever-growing verbatim transcript on every turn instead.

B) The core design flaw is treating a critical, safety-relevant standing fact (the medication interaction) as ordinary conversation history subject to compaction's judgment-based summarization, rather than extracting it into a structurally protected block (e.g., a dedicated system-prompt section for standing safety-relevant facts) the moment it's stated — a block that sits outside the conversation history being compacted and is therefore never at risk of being judged less salient than more recent content. Auto-compaction should still handle the rest of the conversation normally; the fix is specifically about where this one category of fact lives, not abandoning compaction altogether.

C) The core flaw is that the conversation was allowed to run past 30 turns at all; the team should hard-cap all conversations at 15 turns to avoid ever needing compaction.

D) The core flaw is a model capability issue; upgrading to a more capable model would ensure compaction correctly identifies all safety-relevant facts as important, regardless of how they're structured.

Correct — and this scenario is the most serious-stakes version of the "lost in the middle" pattern in this whole set, precisely because the consequence isn't a mediocre report or a wasted subagent token — it's a safety-relevant detail silently failing to survive not one but two compaction cycles.

**Why B is right**

The mechanism failure here is exactly what you predicted when we discussed this: compaction is a *judgment-based* process — each pass decides what's salient enough to preserve, based on the content it's compressing at that moment. The scenario shows this failing not once but twice, which is itself instructive: this isn't a fluke, it's the predictable behavior of a system that treats a critical standing fact as ordinary conversational content competing for "salience" against everything else being said. By turn 34, two rounds of "what seems important right now" judgment have both, independently, concluded that a turn-3 medication detail was less worth preserving than more recent content — even though its relevance to the task never diminished for a single turn of the conversation.

This is the direct, high-stakes confirmation of the principle from your last two messages: a fact like this shouldn't be *subject to* compaction's judgment at all — it should live in a block that sits structurally outside the conversation history being summarized, e.g., a dedicated "standing safety-relevant facts" section of the system prompt, populated the moment such a fact is stated (turn 3) and never touched by subsequent compaction passes, because it was never part of what gets compacted in the first place. The fix B describes is precise and proportionate: it doesn't throw out compaction as a mechanism (which is still the right tool for everything else in a 30+ turn conversation — resolved tangents, exploratory back-and-forth, content genuinely safe to compress) — it specifically pulls out the one category of content where losing it has real consequences, and gives that category a structurally protected home.

**Why the others are wrong**

- **A)** overcorrects into abandoning compaction entirely and reverting to an unbounded, ever-growing verbatim transcript — which reintroduces exactly the cost and dilution problems compaction exists to solve (recall your IT-support scenario: rising latency, and — notably — the *same* "detail present but diluted" failure mode can still occur even in an uncompacted transcript, since presence in the transcript didn't guarantee correct weighting there either). This throws away a generally-working mechanism to avoid one specific, narrower failure mode that has a much more targeted fix.
- **C)** proposes an arbitrary hard cap that doesn't address the actual mechanism at all — a 15-turn cap doesn't protect the medication fact any better; it just forces a premature conversation restart, likely losing context and requiring the person to re-disclose sensitive information, which is a worse user experience for someone already navigating a vulnerable conversation, without fixing the underlying "critical facts aren't protected" design gap. Nothing prevents the same failure at turn 14 instead of turn 34 under this design.
- **D)** repeats the misdiagnosis pattern you've now correctly rejected in essentially every scenario that offered it as bait — treating a structural/architectural gap as a capability problem. A more capable model still performing judgment-based compaction over unstructured conversation history has no more guarantee of correctly weighting a turn-3 detail against everything since than the current model does; capability doesn't create a structural guarantee where none exists by design.

**Pattern to remember, closing out this whole context-management thread**: compaction (automatic or manual) is a *judgment-based* mechanism, and judgment-based mechanisms will occasionally get the call wrong — that's not a bug to be engineered away, it's an inherent property of summarization. For content where getting that judgment wrong carries real consequence — safety-relevant facts, hard constraints, anything where "probably preserved" isn't good enough — the right architecture doesn't try to make the judgment more reliable, it removes the fact from the judgment process entirely by giving it a structurally protected location outside what gets compacted. This is the same underlying discipline as your guardrails material (instructional vs. structural enforcement) and your auth/authz material (instructions vs. enforced boundaries), now applied a third time to context management specifically: for anything genuinely critical, don't rely on a process that's merely *likely* to preserve it — structurally guarantee that it does.
